# User research profile — Pedro Saffi

Used by the `seminar-prep` skill to write the "connection to your work" line in each attendee card. Edit freely; the skill rereads this every run.

## Core themes
- **Securities lending and short-selling supply.** Equity lending markets, recall risk, fees, supply shocks. Lendable inventory, beneficial owners (mutual funds, pensions, insurers).
- **CDS spreads and credit risk.** How short-selling supply/demand affect CDS pricing and the equity-credit link. Substitution between equity shorting and credit-default protection.
- **Short-selling demand.** Borrower behavior, signed flows, informed shorting, ETFs as short-side conduits.
- **Regulation as identification.** Use of regulatory events (NAIC 2010 SSAP 91R insurer reporting requirement, TD9599 Treasury rule on dividend tax arbitrage, short-sale bans, uptick rule) as exogenous shocks.
- **Insurer balance-sheet behavior.** Insurance companies as securities lenders; how reporting / capital rules change their lending intensity.
- **Empirical finance, identification strategies.** IV, DiD, RDD applied in microstructure / asset-pricing / banking settings.

## Methods toolkit
- Panel data with firm-day or firm-month structure
- Instrumental variables driven by regulatory variation
- Difference-in-differences with continuous treatment
- Event studies around regulatory dates
- Randomization inference for inference under small clusters

## Adjacent areas of interest (for hooks even when not directly empirical-finance)
- Asset pricing — funding constraints, limits to arbitrage
- Banking — collateral, runs, regulatory arbitrage
- Corporate finance — capital structure, financial distress
- Macro-finance — credit cycles, systemic risk
- Econometrics — IV with weak instruments, inference with few clusters, RDD bandwidth selection

## How to write the "connection" line
- Pick the most concrete overlap. Don't say "both work on finance." Say "Both use regulatory variation for identification (you: NAIC 2010; them: X)."
- If overlap is methodological (e.g., they're an econometrician), note which of your designs their work could improve.
- If there's no direct overlap, find a *conversation hook* — a substantive question their work raises that touches your themes obliquely.
- Always actionable — a sentence the user could literally say at the start of the meeting.
